## 9. Known caveats

- `desinventar_no_record_year` years are unknown, not flood-free, for DesInventar.
- `county_flood_rate*` uses all of 2000-2019; recompute inside every backtest fold.
- `pct_built` interpolates between 5-year anchors, so months before an anchor use the next anchor (mild look-ahead).
- `pop_density` is the static 2019 census value on every row (look-ahead for 2000-2018).
- DMI anomalies use a 1991-2020 climatology; the rfh_avg period (1989-2018) is not used.
- **Public labels after 2018:** In the PUBLIC table (no EM-DAT) the flood labels thin out after 2018: the Global Flood Database's last flagged year is 2018, DFO's is 2010, and DesInventar has no records in 2017, 2018, 2019 (it is the only public source that reaches 2020-2025). So public floods in 2020-2025 are heavily UNDER-COUNTED: 17 flagged county-months (0.5%) against 732 (6.5%) in 2000-2019. A model trained and tested on the public labels will see a much lower flood rate in the test years than in training, so test-period recall, precision and base-rate comparisons are not trustworthy, and a low test flood count does not mean fewer floods happened. The private table adds EM-DAT, which covers the recent years, but it may not be shared.
- Boundary: every Earth Engine feature uses the OCHA COD-AB land-only county polygons (the `*_cod.csv` exports).
- Lake-edge caveat: the lakes are cut with the Natural Earth 10m outline, which is coarser than the true shore. Some open water stays inside the land-only polygons of Homa Bay, Siaya, Migori and Marsabit, so `lc_water` and `pct_water` there are higher than for a true-shoreline polygon (Homa Bay lc_water 5.1%, pct_water 4.4%; Siaya 3.4% / 3.0%). In Nakuru and Kajiado the extra water is real small lakes that were deliberately kept.
